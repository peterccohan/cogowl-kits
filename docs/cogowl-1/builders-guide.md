# CogOwl 1 Builder's Guide: Power and Torque

> Build a gearbox. Lift a heavy load with one finger. Find out what you pay for it.

Grades 3–5 · 2 sessions × 45 min · 2–3 builders per kit

This is the text of the printed guide (13 letter-size pages plus a data card). Page numbers match the printed booklet.

---

## Page 2 · For the teacher

**Almost everything is in the box.** No prep, no copies. Each group finds a few things in the room, then opens to page 3.

**What students learn:** gears trade speed for strength. A small gear turning a big gear turns slower but twists harder. Gears never make extra power. They only trade it.

**How it runs**
- Session 1: pages 3–8. Build the frame, meet gears, run Test A.
- Session 2: pages 9–12. Tests B, C, D and the challenge.

**How students measure (no rulers or scales)**
- **Found things.** Each group finds 3 loads of different weights, light to heavy (an eraser, a deck of cards, a roll of tape), and about 40 identical counters (pennies, math cubes, LEGO bricks).
- **Effort counters.** The cups come tied on: load cup to the drum, effort cup to the crank wheel. Students add counters to the effort cup until the load rises. In Test A they try all 3 loads, then keep the one that took 9–12 counters. Fewer counters = a stronger gearbox. Loads differ, so groups compare each test to their own Test A.
- **Crank turns.** The load string has a red bead 20 cm above the cup. Students count crank turns until the bead reaches the drum.

Results go on the wipe-off data card, using the marker in the box.

**Safety:** fingers out of the gear teeth while someone turns. Nothing sharp, breakable or wet in the cups. If the frame bends, use a lighter load. Small parts: not for children under 3.

**At the end:** groups use the pack-up checklist on page 12. Expected results and answers are on page 13.

## Page 4 · Build the frame

The frame holds everything in place. It takes about 5 minutes.

1. Hold the **back frame** with its four posts pointing up.
2. Push the 3 **axles** up through holes **A**, **B** and **C** from underneath, so the flat heads stay on the bottom. They should spin freely.
3. Set the **front plate** on the posts so the axles poke through the same holes. The letters face you.
4. Turn the 4 **thumb screws** into the posts by hand. Snug is enough.
5. Spin each axle with your fingers. If one sticks, loosen the screws a little and check that the plates line up.

**Good to know:** you will take the front plate off and put it back many times. That's normal. Engineers rebuild things to test new ideas.

---

## Page 5 · Lesson 1: Gears push gears

A gear is a wheel with teeth. When the teeth of two gears fit together, we say they **mesh**.

**Try it:** take off the front plate. Put a small gear on axle A. Put a medium gear on an axle 3 holes away. Turn the small gear.

**Think:** the small gear turns one way. Which way does the medium gear turn?

Meshed gears always turn in ________ directions. If the axles are too close, the gears jam. Too far, and the teeth miss. The hole rule gets it right every time.

**The hole rule:** add the teeth of the two gears. Divide by 12. That's how many holes apart their axles go.

| Gears | Math | Holes apart |
|---|---|---|
| 12 + 24 | 36 ÷ 12 | 3 |
| 12 + 36 | 48 ÷ 12 | 4 |
| 24 + 36 | 60 ÷ 12 | ____ |

## Page 6 · Lesson 2: Count the turns

Every gear has one tooth with an orange dot. Use the dots to count turns.

- Small gear + medium gear (3 holes apart). Small gear turns: ____
- Small gear + big gear (4 holes apart). Small gear turns: ____

**Try it:** line up both dots so they point at each other. Turn the small gear slowly and count its turns until the big gear's dot comes back to the start.

Each tooth pushes one tooth. The big gear has 36 teeth and the small one has 12, so the small gear goes around ____ times to push all 36 teeth once.

**New words: gear ratio.** How many turns in for one turn out. Small gear to big gear is **3 to 1**, written **3:1**.
**Think:** a 12-tooth gear turns a 24-tooth gear. What is the gear ratio? ____ : 1

## Page 7 · Lesson 3: Twist it

**Torque** is a twisting push. Push a door near its hinge and it barely moves. Push near the edge and it swings easily. The farther from the middle you push, the more twist you get.

**Meet the winch.** Effort cup falls → crank wheel turns → drum turns too (same axle) → load cup rises.

1. Stand the gearbox on its long edge at the **edge of a desk**, front plate facing out, so the cups can hang down. One builder holds it steady.
2. Take the gears off. Slide the **drum** onto the front of axle A, then the **crank wheel**. (Test A only: in Test B the drum moves to axle B.)
3. Let both cups hang. The load cup hangs on one side of the axle and the effort cup on the other side.
4. **Quick test:** gently pull the effort cup down. The load cup should go up. Goes down? Unwind the load string and wind it the other way around the drum, in one neat layer.

---

## Page 8 · Test A: How strong is the winch?

No gears yet. How many counters does it take to lift each load?

1. Put your **lightest load** in the load cup. One builder holds the crank still.
2. Let go of the crank. Add **counters** to the effort cup one at a time. Stop as soon as the load starts to rise. Write the number on your data card.
3. Take the counters out. Do the same with your other 2 loads.
4. **Keep the load that took 9 to 12 counters.** You will use it for Tests B and C. None did? Swap one for a heavier or lighter thing.
5. Empty the effort cup, but leave your load in. Turn the crank by its handle and count turns until the red bead reaches the drum.

**Stuck? Load cup goes down?** Wind its string the other way around the drum (page 7). **Nothing moves, even with lots of counters?** Spin axle A by hand. If it sticks, loosen the thumb screws a little.

**Fill in the blanks**
- When the effort cup goes down, the load cup goes ________.
- The crank wheel and the drum are on the same ________, so they turn together.
- The effort string hangs ____ times farther from the axle than the load string. So the effort cup twists ____ times harder.
- A twisting push is called ________.

## Page 9 · Lesson 4 · Test B: The trade

Now add gears. A small gear turning a big gear makes the big gear slower, but it twists 3 times harder.

1. Front plate off. **Small gear** on axle A, **big gear** on axle B, in the same layer.
2. Front plate back on. Move the **drum** to the front of axle B. Keep the **crank wheel** on A.
3. Turn the crank a little. If the load cup goes *down*, move its string to the other side of the drum. (Remember Lesson 1: meshed gears turn opposite ways.)
4. Run Test B with the same load. How many counters lift it now? Then count crank turns to the red bead.

**Fill in:** with gears, the load took ________ (more / fewer) counters and ________ (more / fewer) crank turns.

**Bike connection:** riding uphill in an easy gear, you pedal a lot but it doesn't feel hard. Your bike is doing the same trade.

---

## Page 10 · Lesson 5 · Test C: Stack the layers

Gears only mesh in the same layer. A shared axle carries the turn to the next layer.

1. **Layer 1 (back):** small gear on A, big gear on B.
2. **Layer 2 (front):** a second small gear on B and a big gear on C. Spacers keep the layers apart, so the big gears pass right over each other.
3. Put the front plate back. Drum on the front of C, crank wheel on A. Check which way the load cup moves.
4. Run Test C with the same load. Count counters, then crank turns to the bead.

**Think:** both gears on axle B are locked to the same axle, so they spin together. Layer 1 makes it 3 times stronger. Layer 2 makes it 3 times stronger again. 3 × 3 = ____. Our gearbox is now ____ : 1.

---

## Page 11 · Lesson 6 · Test D: Nothing for free

Gears made lifting easier. So what did you give up?

**Test D: flip it.** Swap the gears so the big gear is on A and the small gear is on B. Drum on B. Take your load out and put just **9 counters** in the load cup. How many counters does the effort cup need? Then count crank turns to the bead.

Look at your data card. When lifting got easier, we turned the crank ________ (more / fewer) times. Flipped, the load flew up ________ (fast / slow), but lifting got much ________ (easier / harder).

**The big idea:** gears trade speed for strength. They never make extra power. Some of your effort also gets lost to rubbing between parts. That rubbing is called **friction**.

## Page 12 · Challenge: Your turn, engineer

**The 20-counter challenge.** Put 20 counters in the load cup. Your effort cup may hold only **4 counters**. Build a gearbox that still lifts the load, using the **fewest crank turns** you can.

Hint: the medium gears are allowed. Use the hole rule.

**Our best design**
- Gears we used: ________
- Crank turns to the bead: ________

**Words to know**
- **gear:** a wheel with teeth
- **mesh:** when gear teeth fit together
- **axle:** the rod a gear spins on
- **gear ratio:** turns in for one turn out
- **torque:** a twisting push
- **friction:** rubbing that wastes effort
- **load:** the thing you lift
- **effort:** the push you give

## Page 13 · Answer key (for the teacher)

Loads differ, so groups compare effort to their own Test A. Crank turns don't depend on the load, so those should match across the room. Friction pushes real numbers a little high.

| Test | Load | Effort counters | Crank turns |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · winch, no gears | 3 loads | 9–12 for the kept load (A) | about 3 |
| B · 3:1 | their load | about A ÷ 3 (3–4) | about 10 |
| C · 9:1 | their load | about A ÷ 9 (1–2) | about 29 |
| D · flipped | 9 counters | about 9–12 | about 1 |

**Think and fill-in answers**
- p. 5: the opposite way · opposite · 5 holes
- p. 6: 2 turns, 3 turns · 3 times · 2:1
- p. 8: up · axle · 3, 3 · torque
- p. 9: fewer · more
- p. 10: 9 · 9:1
- p. 11: more · fast · harder
- Data card: up · up

**Challenge:** effort must be 5× smaller than the load. The winch alone gives 3×. A 12 → 24 gear pair adds 2×, for 6× total, lifting 20 with about 4 counters in about 6–7 crank turns. 3:1 (9×) also works but takes about 10 turns.

**If something goes wrong**
- **Gears jam or skip:** the axles are the wrong distance apart. Use the hole rule (p. 5).
- **Load goes down:** wind its string the other way around the drum (p. 7).
- **A gear spins on its axle:** make sure the hex hole is fully on the hex part of the axle.
- **Load won't rise with the expected counters:** add one more. That extra counter is friction.

**The math, if students ask:** effort ≈ load ÷ (3 × gear ratio). Crank turns ≈ 3 × gear ratio. The 3 comes from the crank wheel being 3× wider than the drum.
